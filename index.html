About

I am a final-year Computer Engineering student at the National University of Singapore (NUS), graduating in June 2027. I work in the Xtra group with Prof. Bingsheng He, where I lead my final-year project on long-term memory for LLM agents. Before that, I was a research intern at Huawei's Singapore Search & Recommendation Lab, working on reinforcement learning for geometric generation.

My research asks how LLM agents can keep a useful long-term memory at low cost: how memory can be built and searched with fewer model calls, without losing the evidence that later questions depend on. I am also interested in efficient LLM inference and in reinforcement learning from feedback.

  • Long-term memory for LLM agents
  • Cost–quality trade-offs in LLM systems
  • Reinforcement learning from feedback

Research

PlexusMem: Efficient Long-Term Memory for Conversational Agents

Jun 2026 – present

Final Year Project, Xtra Group, NUS · Supervisor: Prof. Bingsheng He

PlexusMem studies the cost–quality trade-off of long-term memory for conversational agents. It asks whether memory can be built and searched with fewer LLM tokens while keeping the evidence needed for later questions. The prototype links each memory unit to its source, organizes memory in a recursive hierarchy and connects related units with rule-based associations. I lead the project's research design, and I designed, ran and analyzed all experiments: comparisons with existing memory systems, token-cost breakdowns, ablations and evidence-coverage diagnostics.

PyGeoX: Reinforcement Learning for Geometric Generation

Dec 2025 – Jun 2026

Research Intern, Huawei Singapore Search & Recommendation Lab

I ran model fine-tuning and reinforcement learning experiments for geometric generation and analyzed the results. With the first author, I built and validated PyGeoX-Bench, curating and checking Chinese entrance-examination mathematics problems for real-world evaluation. I wrote the paper's experimental section and helped review and revise the manuscript.

Autoregressive LLM Sampling and Interpretability

Huawei internship

I explored Sequential Monte Carlo decoding as a way to reduce regeneration after late-stage rejection, comparing its time–memory trade-offs with a Monte Carlo baseline. I also studied whether hidden-state model confidence can decide when to accept or reject a sample during generation.
